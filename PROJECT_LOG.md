# COMETO — Project Log

Running log. Newest entry at the bottom of the Changelog; the sections above it are
kept current so the project can be understood from this file alone.
Last updated: 2026-11-15 (AAA aesthetic passes 1–5).

## 1. What this is

COMETO is an original 2D handheld science-fiction RPG (Godot 4.7.2, GDScript only,
Mobile renderer, iPhone first, landscape). Setting: Cometographia. First location:
Anjabi, a coastal mining settlement. Repo root: `~/cometo`. It replaces the earlier
3D first-person direction in `~/cometographia-2`; no code or assets are shared.

Status: early playable prototype. One detailed map (the Collapsed Mine, location 04)
with a short investigation and one environmental puzzle, plus a walkable greybox of
the whole town built from the director's layout. All art is provisional. No combat,
no audio, no companions yet.

## 2. Run, test, package

```bash
GODOT=/Applications/Godot_mono-1.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot
$GODOT --path .                                              # play
$GODOT --headless --path . --import                          # after adding scripts or assets
$GODOT --headless --path . res://tests/slice_test.tscn       # end-to-end test (55 checks), exit 0 = pass
$GODOT --path . res://tests/screenshot.tscn -- /some/folder  # windowed capture of the mine map
$GODOT --path . res://tests/screenshot_town.tscn -- /some/folder
$GODOT --headless --path . -s tools/build_town_mask.gd       # rebuild town walkable mask from the greybox
sh tools/package_review.sh                                   # export/COMETO_review_<date>.zip
```

Controls: WASD / arrows move, E / Space / Enter interact and advance dialogue,
J or Tab journal, Esc menu. Touch or mouse: drag anywhere to steer, tap to interact,
tap the prompt chip, JOURNAL and MENU chips top right.

### `core/` — autoloads and shared logic (no drawing)
| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `game_state.gd` | Autoload `GameState`. Flat flag dictionary = persistent world state. Defines the condition language (`check`) and effect language (`apply`) used by dialogue, interactables and puzzles. Signals: `flag_changed`, `notified` (HUD toast), `travel_requested` (map change), `location_entered` (title card). |
| `journal.gd` | Autoload `Journal`. Investigations (quests with hidden/active/done objectives) and clues. Definitions come from `data/journal`; this holds progress only. |
| `dialogue.gd` | Autoload `Dialogue`. Runs dialogue JSON: entry conditions, nodes, lines, choices, effects. Emits signals; draws nothing. Data format documented at the top of the file. |
| `save_manager.gd` | Autoload `SaveManager`. JSON saves in `user://save_<slot>.json`, written atomically. Slot `manual` from the menu; slot `auto` written when the app is backgrounded or closed and resumed on boot. |

## 4. How the systems connect

- Everything that must persist is a flag in `GameState` or an entry in `Journal`.
  Maps are reloaded from scratch on travel and on load, and each node reads its
  state in `_ready`. That is the whole save model.
- Dialogue, examine text, puzzle feedback and NPC reactions are all dialogue JSON
  using the same conditions and effects. A clue found by a puzzle can unlock a
  dialogue choice, and a dialogue choice can reveal an objective.
- Maps come in two kinds. Detailed maps (`mine.tscn`) use collision bodies.
  Plan maps (`town.tscn`) use a painted image plus a `WalkMask`.
- Travel: a `MapExit` emits `GameState.travel_requested`; `game/main.gd` loads the
  target map and places the player at the named spawn.

## 5. Anjabi layout decisions

- Greybox Study 01 is canonical. Town scale is 2 world px per greybox px
  (world 2768 x 1812 inside the frame).
- Walkable in town: beige (walkways, beach, docks) and green (vegetation).
  Not walkable: grey (cliffs, buildings) and water. The mine yard and airpad are
  grey slabs, so their polygons are hand-listed in `tools/build_town_mask.gd` and
  are approximate. Green being walkable lets the player bypass some stairs; it is
  one constant (`GREEN_WALKABLE`).
- Elevation is not modelled. The Imperial Tower is unreachable on foot (tram only).
- The mine map follows location 04: stairs down from the Estate in the north-west,
  a fenced inner yard under the Estate cliff holding the mine door, an outer yard
  with wash-house and sheds, a path east to the airpad, a cliff drop to the south.
  Its internal arrangement (gate, spur line, washout) is the prototype's own.

## 6. Provenance of content

- From the director: the world premise, the three cultures, Anjabi and its sixteen
  named locations, both map images, the art and gameplay direction.
- Written for the prototype and NOT approved lore: the investigation's specifics
  (crew recalled early, yard power cut, door welded from outside, unopened

  so no character names have been invented.

## 7. Proposed, not built

Three combat directions were proposed; none is chosen or implemented.
1. Live Ground — fights on the exploration map itself, real-time movement in small
   zones, abilities reshape terrain; field abilities and combat abilities are the
   same verbs. Recommended; hardest to balance.
2. Signal & Body — a turn-based physical row plus a real-time signal lane where an
   operator intercepts enemy actions before they resolve.
3. Tempo Line — turn-based on a shared timeline with a short positional strip per
   side; pushes and interrupts move enemies in time as well as space.

## 8. Known limitations and untested areas

- 3D (entry 4, updated entry 8): every building is an authored Blender model. Terrain,
  cliffs, fences, rails, props, trees and characters are still primitives built in code. No AnimationPlayer; the walk is procedural. No camera occlusion
  handling yet (nothing tall stands south of a walkable area so far). Only the
  every map is 3D. The town is a 3D greybox: walkways are smooth slopes from the height
  interpolation, not modelled stairs, and its buildings are not enterable. The district
  and the town both contain the Steps, tavern and pods (the district is the detailed
  version of the town's centre); they are not yet merged. The district's mine approach
  and the mine yard both show a gate.
- Superseded 2D files still in the repo, no longer loaded: `maps/anjabi/mine.tscn`,
  `mine_ground.gd`, `game/puzzle/ore_cart.gd`, `track_switch.gd`, `maps/anjabi/town.tscn`,
  `town.gd`, `town_greybox.png` (still read by the tools). With no 2D maps left,
  `Player`, `Npc`, `Interactable`, `MapExit`, `GameMap`, `WalkMask`, `CharSprite`,
  `ProvBlock`, `ProvRail` and the 2D branches in `main.gd` are unused at runtime.
- No inventory, equipment, stats, skill checks, reputation, combat or classes
  exist in the project; none were preserved or added.
- iPhone performance has not been measured; no device or export was available. After the
  optimisation pass the desktop proxy shows 103–221 draw calls and 43–163k primitives per
  frame (`tests/perf_probe.tscn`).
- Batched and merged scenery (pass 9) is fixed once built: models in
  `MapBuilder3D.BATCHED` return null from `model()`, and builder primitives under plain
  nodes are merged one frame after `_ready`. Anything that must move or hide later has to
  live under a scripted node (as doors, NPCs and puzzle pieces already do).

- Touch: only the steering vector is tested. Real drag and tap gestures, the
  safe-area margins and gamepad input have never been tried on a device.
- iOS build not yet produced (entry 10). Preset, icon and mobile settings are ready and
  Godot accepts the preset; blocked on this Mac by: no iOS export templates installed,
  no Xcode (command-line tools only), macOS 13.7.8 (current Xcode needs 14.5+), no Apple
  signing team. iOS export from the .NET Godot build is experimental; this project is
  GDScript only, so use the standard Godot 4.7.2 build and its templates.
- Coast kit (entries 28, 29): everything comes from the one reference picture. Modular
  houses have one wall colour, one door and porch per roof, two windows and two planters;
  roofs are plain rectangles (no gables or L shapes); the shoreline follows the tile grid
  in steps; there are no cliffs, stairs, bridges or interiors; the chibi ignores the
  created character's colours; no people but the player.
  Anjabi's own maps are still at the 32 px scale.
- Boardwalk map (entry 27): the water is a stand-in drawn in code; the tram line is
  pylons only (no north-south track or cable art); the ways out are signs, not joined
  to other maps; no people; the station has no interior.
- Anjabi tileset v0.1 (entry 25): 17 tiles carry a cleanup flag (list in entry 25). The
  player and NPC sheets are still the 16x32 placeholders, drawn at 2x on 32 px maps, so
  their pixels are twice as coarse as the tiles. Only grass has edge transitions. The
  16 px slice (Steps, shop) and the 32 px yard are separate maps at different scales.
- `tests/slice2d_test.gd` "a tap turns in place" fails about half the time headless (a
  2-frame tap against a 0.09 s threshold); it does so on commit 6d77825 too.
- No audio. No combat. No companions. No NPC routines yet (the clock and calendar they would use exist, entry 13).
- The town is a greybox: no NPCs or interactables there except the two mine exits.
- The town's mine yard and the detailed mine map represent the same place twice.
- Save files from before the mine map was renamed fall back to the start map.
- Dialogue box is taller than it needs to be for single lines.

### Pass 9 — optimisation (2026-11-15)
- Figures: Godot's primitive defaults (64 segments, 32 rings) made every eye, button and
  joint thousands of triangles; `Figure3D._lower_poly()` caps segments by size. Each
  limb's parts are then merged into one vertex-coloured mesh (cloth/skin surface, metal
  surface; the emissive hard-hat lamp stays separate). 101k → 4.6k triangles and ~50 → ~6
  draws per figure. Same segment caps on the ore cart wheels, switch lever and puddles.
- Pines, lantern posts, barrels, planters and crate stacks are no longer instanced:
  `model()` routes them to `batch()`, which builds one MultiMesh per model part per 24 m
  cell (pines and lanterns cast shadows, the rest do not).
- `scatter()` (ferns, flowers, boulders, clutter) and `railings()` are split per 24 m cell

## 13. Sprint, interface restyle, world time and weather (2026-11-15)

### Movement and animation
- Sprint: hold Shift (pad B or X), or tap the HUD's RUN toggle on touch. `Player3D.run_speed`
  7.4 m/s against a 4.2 m/s walk. Input action `sprint` in `core/input_setup.gd`.
- `Figure3D` now has knees, elbows and a neck joint (two-segment limbs, head on its own
  pivot). One gait phase drives thigh swing, knee fold on the forward leg, counter-swinging
  bent arms, torso lean and twist, level head; `_run` blends walk into run (longer stride,
  higher knees, pumping arms, more lean and bounce). Idle: breathing, arm sway, the head
  glances about. Applies to NPCs and townsfolk as well.
- Running kicks up spray at the heels; the camera leads 1.6 m and widens 2.5 degrees.

### Interface
- `UiTheme` rewritten: deep blue translucent fields, pale cyan accent (`ACCENT`, replacing
  `AMBER`), cool white text, spaced capitals for labels (`caps_font()`), DIN-style face.
- Panels use `TechPanelStyle` (hairline outline, corner brackets). Chips are underlined
  capitals. Compass is a hairline instrument ring. Quest tracker is a small header, a rule
  and dash-marked objectives.

### World time (Phase 1)
- `WorldTime` autoload: 24-hour clock, 1 real second = 1 game minute (`minutes_per_second`),
  midnight advances `WorldCalendar` (7-day week, 4 seasons of 28 days, years). New games
  start Autumn 12, Thursday, 6:40 PM (`WorldTime.start`).
- Periods (configurable `periods`): dawn 05, morning 07, midday 11, afternoon 15, evening
  18, night 20. Signals: `minute_changed`, `hour_changed`, `day_changed`, `season_changed`,
  `year_changed`, `period_changed`, `time_jumped`, `event_due`.
- Stops with the scene tree (journal, menu), during dialogue, and for `pause(reason)`.
  `advance_minutes`, `sleep_until`, `set_time`, `set_date`, `is_between(a, b)`.
- Debug builds: `]` +1 hour, `[` +1 day, `\` cycle weather.

### Day and night (Phase 2)
- `EnvironmentController` (one per map, made in `MapBuilder3D._environment()`): samples sky,
  ambient, sun, fill and fog colours from Gradients and energies, exposure and lamp state
  from Curves, every frame from the continuous clock. One shadowed light is sun by day and
  moon by night; it changes sides at dawn and dusk while its energy is zero.
- Street lamps (`lamp_lights` group) are lit dusk to dawn and under heavy cloud.
- `GameMap3D.indoor` / `controller.indoor` uses `indoor_look` and ignores sky and weather.

### Weather (Phase 3)
- `Weather` autoload: clear, cloudy, rain, heavy rain, fog, snow. Spells of 3 to 9 game
  hours are planned a day ahead from per-season weights (`season_weights`); `forecast()`,
  `set_weather(type, minutes)`, `clear_override()`, `lightning` signal for thunder.
- Presentation blends over about 10 s: sun and sky dim and grey with cloud, fog density,
  rain and splash density, snow particles, ground dries and puddles shrink
  (`weather_wet`), snow settles on level ground (`weather_snow`); both are global shader
  parameters read by `wet_ground.gdshader`. Lightning flashes the sky in heavy rain.
- One set of particle emitters on the player is reused for all weather (`Rain3D`).

### HUD and persistence (Phase 4)
- `WorldClockHUD` top right: "AUTUMN 12 · THURSDAY" over "6:42 PM" with a weather glyph.
  Hidden in dialogue and when `WorldTime.hud_hidden` is set.
- Saves carry `world_time` (date, time, rate) and `weather` (current, schedule, override,
  RNG state). Loading lights the world for its time at once (`snap()`), no replay.
  Older saves without these start at the default date.

### Testing (Phase 5)
- Slice test 142/142: midnight, season and year rollover, long skips, sleeping, events,
  pausing, forecast determinism, change frequency, overrides, seasonal tendencies, day and
  night lighting, smoothness, weather blending, indoor override, save and load, sprint.
- `tests/screenshot_3d` and `tests/perf_probe` are pinned to 21:30 rain so they stay
  comparable. Desktop cost after this entry: 135-298 draw calls, 54-179k primitives,
  4-5 ms per frame (the interface's antialiased lines account for most of the added draws).

### Limits
- Snow settles on terrain and paving only; roofs and props stay bare.
- No audio, so thunder and ambience are signals only.
- No interiors exist yet; the indoor override is tested by toggling it on the town.
- Sunset and sunrise times are fixed (they do not vary by season).
- Not run on an iPhone; the web and Mac builds in `export/` predate this entry.

## 15. 2D prototype, Golden Sun style (2026-11-15)

A test of turning the game into 2D top-down (3/4 view) in the vein of Golden Sun. The 3D
game is unchanged; the prototype is one extra map reachable from the title (2D PROTOTYPE).

### How it is made
- `tools/bake_2d.gd` renders the existing 3D Steps district (the standalone district map:
  plaza, tavern terrace, pods, mine approach, coast) with an orthographic camera at 45
  degrees, stretched so a point (x, y, z) lands at (x, z - y): ground squares stay square
  and walls stand up. 16 px per metre.
- Flat things (paving, walls, stairs, sea) go into one pre-rendered ground image with
  the shadows of the tall things on it. Every model (tavern, shop, pods, lamps, trees,
  barrels, tables, banners) is rendered alone as a sprite, with its signs. Emissive
  parts (windows, signs, lamp glass) are rendered again on their own as glow layers.
- Heights: the 3D colliders are ray-cast every 1/4 m into a height grid. The 2D map
  walks on it: no ground or the sea blocks, a step over 0.35 m blocks. So terrace
  walls, railings, buildings and the drop to the beach block, and stairs climb.
- Characters: the in-game figure is rendered in 8 directions, idle plus 8 walk and 8
  run frames, downscaled with a hard edge and a one-pixel dark outline.

### How it plays
- Free 8-direction movement, walk 4.2 m/s, hold Shift (or RUN) to sprint 7.4 m/s, with
  slide along walls. Camera at 2x zoom (about 15 by 8 m, the handheld proportion),
  snapped to whole art pixels.
- Everything is y-sorted by where it stands, so the player passes behind the tavern,
  lamps and trees and in front of them. Height lifts sprites on the terrace and pods.
- The tavern keeper, the pod B-17 door, the mine warning sign and the exit to the 3D mine
  yard all work through the existing Interactable and dialogue data.
- Day, night and weather from the same WorldTime and Weather: a CanvasModulate tint
  sampled from the 3D gradients, lamps as PointLight2D, windows and signs glow after
  dark, screen-space rain, snow and mist. Saves and loads like any map.

### Tests
- Slice test 154/154 (12 new: heights, walking, the terrace wall, the stairs, the keeper,
  night tint, save and load). Menu test 42/42.

### Limits
- Pre-rendered, not hand-drawn pixel art: the look is "rendered 3D sprites", and the
  cobble texture is noisy at 16 px per metre. Final art would replace ground.png and the
  sprites on the same layout.
- The player's sheet is the default look; a created character's look is not baked.
- Townsfolk walk without the height rules; the keeper is the only NPC in this area.
- Bake is a manual step (re-run the tool after changing the district); output 6.1M
  in `art/baked2d/`.
- Signs on buildings are drawn with the building, so its sort applies to them.
- Untested on an iPhone or in a browser; builds in `export/` predate this entry.

## 23. 2D rebuild: the Steps vertical slice (2026-11-15, branch `2d`)

Baseline: tag `3d-final` (the 3D game as of entry 22); branch `2d` created from it. The 3D
implementation is untouched and still runs (its tests pass). Nothing was deleted.

### Architecture (logic independent of rendering)
- **Data**: maps are JSON (`data/maps2d/`): four character-grid layers (ground, build,
  detail, over) and a list of objects (props with footprints, flat decor, NPCs by id,
  walkers, door warps, signs, lights) and spawns. NPCs are defined in
  `data/npcs2d.json`. Dialogue, quests, clues, saves, the clock and weather are the
  existing systems, unchanged.
- **World** (`world2d/`): `TileWorld` builds TileMapLayers and answers gameplay
  questions (can enter, what is on that tile, where does this door go). `GridActor`,
  `GridPlayer`, `GridNpc` move tile by tile and know nothing about how art is made.
- **Art** (`render2d/pixel_art.gd`): the only place names become pictures. Placeholder
  tiles, props and characters are generated in code in the settlement palette; a PNG in
  `art/pixel/tiles|props|chars/<name>.png` replaces any of them with no code change.
- **View**: tile maps render in their own SubViewport at native pixel size (240 px, 15
  tiles wide; about 15 x 8 tiles at the 480x270 reference), scaled up 2x inside the
  interface, then by a whole number to the window (`stretch/scale_mode = integer`), with
  2D transforms snapped to pixels. Taller windows simply show more rows.
- `main.gd` routes `TileWorld` maps to the 2D view and spawns a `GridPlayer`; the title,
  character creation, menus, HUD, journal, dialogue box, clock, saves and travel all work
  unchanged. `App.start_map` makes NEW GAME start on the 2D Steps; tests that build
  `main.tscn` directly still start in the 3D town.

### The slice
- **Movement**: FireRed/HeartGold style. Tap a direction to turn in place, hold to walk
  (0.24 s per tile, gliding), hold run to run (0.13 s per tile), steps chain with no pause,
  walking into a wall plays a bump. Four directions, idle, two-frame-per-step walk and
  run cycles; 16x32 adult figures. Touch drag and the RUN toggle map to the same.
- **The Steps** (40 x 30 tiles): the tavern (ochre), two townhouses (rose; teal with a
  verdigris dome) along the terrace top; a garden with a tree, cypress and bench; the
  terrace with a stone balustrade; the arcade wall below with six shopfronts, striped
  awnings and banners; the central stairs under a stone arch; the plaza with a brass
  compass rose and the armillary landmark, two market stalls with merchants and a rug,
  a public terminal, lamps, benches, string lights, lavender; the shop with its awning
  and lit windows; a balustrade over the sea wall to the south; arched passages west and
  east (roads beyond the slice, placeholder signs).
- **Depth**: props and people share a y-sorted layer (behind a lamp when north of it, in
  front when south); eaves and arches are on an overhead layer drawn above the player.
- **NPC and dialogue**: the tavern keeper (data-defined) turns to face you; the existing
  dialogue runs in the existing dialogue box.
- **Quest step**: asking the keeper the way to the mine records a new clue,
  `mine_directions` (the keeper's own words), in the journal. This is the slice's quest
  interaction; it also applies in the 3D game.

## 25. Anjabi tileset v0.1: TileSet library and test yard (2026-11-16)

The director supplied the first Anjabi pixel art (seven images; the two ZIP archives
named in the brief did not arrive, so the images as attached are the source). Imported,
prepared and built into a reusable library and a playable test scene. No existing map,
system or test was changed except one line in `game/main.gd` (the map list).

### Decisions
- **32 px tiles.** The slice is 16 px tiles with 16x32 people in a 240 px view. The new
  art carries about twice that detail, so the library is 32 px as briefed. A 32 px map
  (`TilesetWorld`) asks main's 2D view for the full 480x270 (15 x 8.4 tiles, the same
  tile count as the slice) and draws the placeholder people at 2x, one tile wide.
  Leaving the map restores the 240 px view.
- **Scale.** Sprites are reduced to half source size (the art's own pixels are 3-4 source
  px), then palette-reduced with hard alpha, so nothing is filtered at run time. A
  length a little over whole cells is shrunk to fit (never below 0.44); modular walls
  are fitted to exact cell widths. Doors come out about 35 x 48 px.
- **Ground.** Each texture is resampled on its own pixel grid (6 to 12 source px per art
  pixel), flattened so a small tile does not repeat as blotches, and cut into one
  self-wrapping 32 px tile (min-cut seams) plus three variants that share its border,
  so any variant sits next to any other.
- **Multi-cell objects** are single atlas tiles placed by their bottom-left cell; the
  solid cells are tile custom data (`solid_cells`) and matching physics polygons. The
  grid movement reads the custom data; the polygons are there for physics later.
- **Depth.** Ground, Edges and Terrain are flat layers. Nature, Architecture, Industrial
  and Props are y-sorted layers inside `Entities` with the people, so anyone north of
  an object's foot is drawn behind it.
- **Grass edges.** The art has no transition tiles, so 19 grass fringe tiles are cut from
  the grass tile itself (ragged edge, 1 px shadow) and laid over neighbouring materials.

### The library (177 tiles)
Ground 16, Terrain 51, Nature 14, Architecture 24, Industrial 35, Props 37.

### Flagged for manual cleanup (also printed by the builder, and in each tile's `flag`)
- Diagonal (isometric) pieces that do not match the front-on buildings: `stairs_diagonal`,
  `plank_bridge_b`, `roof_slab_ac`, `roof_ac_unit`, `roof_corner`, `stairwell_down`,
  `guideway_curve`.
- `train`: three-quarter view on side-on track; it does not sit on the rails convincingly.
- `platform_canopy`: one sprite, so nobody can walk under it; needs posts and an overhead
  layer. `turnstiles`: solid; passing needs the sprite split and a gate.
- `cliff_overhang_a`, `cliff_overhang_b`: irregular; they do not join `cliff_face`
  (which was made to repeat left-right). There are no cliff ends or corners.
- `giant_tree_base`: cut off flat at the top.
- `wall_window_small` is 1.5 cells wide; `fence_gate` was stretched a third to match the
  fence; `walkway_*` pieces do not join on the grid; prefab wall modules have their own
  end caps and read as separate units side by side.
- Not flagged per tile: the terrain patches (dirt, mud, puddles, paving) are 2 x 2
  decals with leafy borders, not tiling ground, and their brown earth does not match the
  vivid plain red-dirt texture. The interior floor panels are framed swatches.

### Tests
- New `tests/anjabi_tileset_test.gd`: 61/61.
- Existing: slice 162/162, menu 42/42. slice2d 34/34 on a good run; see the flaky check
  in section 8.

### Limits
- No interior scene uses the interior pieces yet. No night glow layers for the new art
  (lamp tiles do cast lamplight). The test yard has no NPCs, signs or doors.
- Rebuilding with `tools/build_anjabi_tileset.gd` overwrites the `.tres` files and the
  test scene; copy the scene before painting in the editor.
